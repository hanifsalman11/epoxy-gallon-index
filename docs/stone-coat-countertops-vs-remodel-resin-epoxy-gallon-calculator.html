Frequently Asked Questions (Stone Coat Countertops Vs Remodel Resin)

How many gallons of epoxy do I need for Stone Coat Countertops Vs Remodel Resin?

For a 72" x 30" x 0.125" surface, net volume is 270.0 cubic inches (270 / 231 = 1.17 gallons). Adding a necessary 10% buffer for live-edge roll-off, container cling, and surface tension brings the total to 1.29 Gallons (5.2 Quarts, or 165 Fluid Ounces). A 2-gallon epoxy kit is recommended to provide sufficient volume.

Should I use 1:1 Table Top or 2:1 Deep Pour epoxy for Stone Coat Countertops Vs Remodel Resin?

You must use a 1:1 self-leveling table-top epoxy. Thin countertop resurfacing applications (1/8" / 0.125") require rapid exothermic crosslinking kinetics to self-level and cure hard within 24 to 48 hours. A slow-curing 2:1 or 3:1 deep pour epoxy poured at 1/8" lacks the mass to drive exothermic polymerization, remaining soft, tacky, and highly susceptible to dust contamination.

How do I prevent bubbles and yellowing in Stone Coat Countertops Vs Remodel Resin?

Prevent outgassing bubbles by pre-sealing any porous substrate with a thin brushed seal coat 4-6 hours prior to the flood coat, followed by rhythmic torch sweeps at 15-minute intervals. Prevent amber discoloration and UV yellowing by choosing a resin formulated with both UV absorbers and Hindered Amine Light Stabilizers (HALS), and by avoiding excess heat exposure that can scorch the polymer.
